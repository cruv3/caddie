## TL;DR (my words, after reading)

Argues that context-aware systems cannot fully sense human intent and so must remain *intelligible* (users see what the system "believes") and keep humans *accountable* for consequential actions. Lays out four design principles that became the seed of today's "explain / confirm / control" patterns. Original source of the three intervention principles (correct / confirm / choose).

## Direct quotes

> "Context-aware systems must be intelligible as to their states, 'beliefs,' and 'initiatives' if users are to govern their behavior." — paper

## Key concepts / terms

- **Intelligibility** — the system's beliefs and initiatives are visible to the user. (source: paper)
- **Accountability** — the user remains in control of consequential actions. (source: paper)
- **Correct / Confirm / Choose** — the three intervention modes (full quote reproduced in Hardian 2006 §3 p.2). (source: paper)
